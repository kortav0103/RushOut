# 1.1 Problem Analysis & Requirements Elicitation

## 1.1.1 Problem Statement & Project Motivation

Sliding-block traffic puzzles (popularized by *Rush Hour* and *Parking Jam*) are among the most enduring casual brain-training genres on mobile platforms due to their intuitive spatial rules and high cognitive replayability. However, an analysis of existing mobile offerings reveals five recurring pain points that degrade the player experience:

1. **Intrusive Monetization & Broken Flow State:** Most free-to-play puzzle apps inject forced interstitial video ads after every 1–2 levels, interrupting concentration and discouraging short, focused play sessions.
2. **Suboptimal Drag Physics & Weak Tactile Feedback:** Many implementations snap vehicles abruptly cell-by-cell during touch gestures or couple physics directly to UI state rebuilds, resulting in choppy frame rates, lack of collision feedback, and imprecise control on smaller screens.
3. **Unreliable or Paywalled Hint Systems:** Several puzzle clones either lock hints behind microtransactions/ads or provide heuristic hints that do not guarantee the mathematically shortest path, frustrating players trying to achieve optimal move counts.
4. **Lack of Structured Progression & Social Retention:** Purely offline puzzle packs lack daily engagement hooks, while online-only games fail completely during commutes or in low-connectivity environments.
5. **Accessibility Gaps:** Relying solely on vehicle color to distinguish the target car or movement direction creates friction for players with color-vision deficiency (CVD).

### Project Vision & Scope
**RushOut** is a 2D top-down sliding-block puzzle mobile application built in **Flutter** using **Clean Architecture** and **BLoC/Cubit**. Its objective is to deliver a distraction-free, **60–120 FPS** tactile puzzle experience featuring:
- **4,050 curated campaign levels** (1,350 Easy, 1,350 Medium, 1,350 Hard) parsed from standardized 36-character Rush Hour boards, ordered via a 3-zone **Commercial Sawtooth Pacing** pipeline (`LevelOrderingPipeline`), with deterministic star ratings (`1–3` stars), a **Skip Level** option, and automatic in-progress state persistence.
- A **Level #1 Solver-Driven Interactive Tutorial** (`TutorialService` + `GameCubit`) with welcome rules, glowing target slots, and locked-vehicle guidance for first-time players.
- A **pure-Dart Breadth-First Search (BFS) Hint Solver** executed off the UI thread via background isolates (`Isolate.run`) to compute exact shortest-path hints without frame drops.
- An **Offline-First Daily Challenge & Top-20 Global Leaderboard** synchronized with a **Supabase PostgreSQL** backend and cached locally in **SQLite**, plus zero-friction **Device-ID Cloud Progress Sync** (`PlayerIdentityService`).
- Integrated **audio-haptic feedback** (BGM + pooled SFX + boundary collision haptics) and **color-blind-friendly directional overlays** (`↔` / `↕`) inside a unified **Daylight Tactile UI** with a high-contrast dark asphalt parking board.

## 1.1.3 User Personas

### Persona 1: Minh Anh — The Daily Commuter (Casual Player)
- **Demographics:** 21 years old, Undergraduate Student in Ho Chi Minh City, uses a mid-range Android phone.
- **Usage Context:** Plays for 5–10 minutes on the bus or between lectures, often with unstable mobile data or in Airplane Mode.
- **Goals:** Unwind with quick, satisfying brain teasers without draining battery or mobile data; resume a hard puzzle later if interrupted by a bus stop.
- **Frustrations:** *"I hate when a puzzle app forces me to watch a 30-second ad after a 20-second level, or loses my progress when I switch apps to reply to a message."*
- **Mapped Needs:** Offline SQLite campaign progression, in-progress board persistence, portrait-locked one-handed ergonomics, quick mute toggle.

### Persona 2: Hoàng Nam — The Optimization Enthusiast (Competitive Player)
- **Demographics:** 27 years old, Software Engineer, uses both Android and iOS devices.
- **Usage Context:** Plays in the evening for 15–30 minutes; enjoys competing on daily leaderboards and achieving the theoretical minimum move count (`optimalMoves`).
- **Goals:** Clear all Hard levels with 3 stars; solve the Daily Challenge in minimum moves and see his name in the Top 20; verify that hints actually lead to optimal solutions.
- **Frustrations:** *"On some games, dragging a car feels laggy, and the leaderboard is full of hackers submitting 1-move scores."*
- **Mapped Needs:** 60–120 FPS continuous pixel dragging with smooth snapping, Undo/Reset stack, BFS shortest-path hint solver, Daily Challenge synced with Supabase and protected by Row Level Security (RLS).

### Persona 3: Thảo Vy — The Mindful Evening Player (Accessibility-Focused)
- **Demographics:** 34 years old, Office Administrator, has mild red-green color-vision deficiency (deuteranomaly).
- **Usage Context:** Plays before sleep in a dimly lit room with sound turned off or very low.
- **Goals:** Clearly distinguish the target vehicle and understand which vehicles move horizontally vs. vertically at a glance without straining her eyes.
- **Frustrations:** *"Bright white backgrounds hurt my eyes at night, and when all cars look similar in shape, I waste moves trying to figure out which way a truck slides."*
- **Mapped Needs:** Daylight Tactile UI with high-contrast dark asphalt parking board, distinct pink target sports car sprite + directional badges (`↔` / `↕`) on every vehicle, interactive Level #1 tutorial & How-to-Play visual guide, persistent mute state.

## 1.1.4 Requirements Elicitation Evidence

To ground RushOut's feature set in empirical user needs rather than assumptions, three elicitation activities were conducted: **(A) Competitor App Analysis**, **(B) Quantitative User Survey**, and **(C) Qualitative Semi-Structured Interviews**.

### A. Competitor App Analysis

Three leading mobile apps in the sliding-block / parking puzzle category were evaluated on Android/iOS against key user-experience and technical criteria:

| Evaluation Criterion | *Unblock Me* (Kiragames) | *Parking Jam 3D* (Popcore) | *Rush Hour®* (ThinkFun) | **RushOut (Target Solution)** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Visual Style & Clarity** | 2D wooden blocks; functional but visually dated | 3D isometric; visually busy, camera angle can obscure gaps | Classic 2D board; clean but minimal feedback | **Daylight Tactile UI with 2D top-down dark asphalt board**, high-contrast vehicle sprites & hazard exit bay |
| **Monetization / Ads** | Frequent banner & interstitial ads | Heavy forced video ads every 1–2 levels | Paid unlocks for extra packs | **Ad-free academic/indie build**; uninterrupted puzzle flow |
| **Drag Physics & Feel** | Basic grid snapping; minimal collision feedback | Tap-to-slide (no continuous manual drag control) | Grid-step drag; occasional stutter | **60–120 FPS continuous pixel drag** (`ValueNotifier`), 100ms ease-out snap, haptic + horn collision feedback |
| **Hint System** | Limited free hints; requires ad/IAP | Minimal or paid skips | Step-by-step hints, limited supply | **Exact BFS Shortest-Path Solver** running on background Isolate (3 free hints + 3-star bonus) |
| **Online & Social Features** | Basic level stars; no daily global board | Artificial/bot-heavy leagues | Offline-focused | **Real Daily Challenge + Top-20 Global Leaderboard + Device-ID Cloud Sync** (Supabase) with UTC sync & offline SQLite fallback |
| **Accessibility** | Color-coded blocks only | Color & 3D orientation | Color-coded cars | **Directional badges (`↔` / `↕`)** on every vehicle + Daylight Tactile high-contrast palette + Interactive Tutorial & How-to-Play guide |

**Key Takeaway:** Competitors force a trade-off between *smooth modern polish* (*Parking Jam 3D*, which sacrifices manual sliding precision and floods users with ads) and *pure Rush Hour logic* (*Unblock Me*, which lacks modern tactile polish, daily social competition, and accessibility cues). RushOut bridges this gap.

### B. Quantitative User Survey ($N = 24$ Respondents)

An online questionnaire was distributed to 24 university students and casual mobile gamers (aged 19–32) who play puzzle games at least twice a week.

| Survey Question | Top Findings & Distribution | Elicitation Insight |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Q1. Typical session duration?** | **62.5%** (15/24) play for **5–15 minutes**; **25.0%** (6/24) play <5 minutes; **12.5%** (3/24) play >15 minutes. | App must launch quickly, support instant resume, and save unfinished board states automatically. |
| **Q2. Biggest frustration with mobile puzzle games?** | **79.2%** (19/24) cited **excessive ads**; **54.2%** (13/24) cited **laggy/clunky controls**; **37.5%** (9/24) cited **getting stuck with no fair hint**. | Prioritize zero-interruption flow, 60–120 FPS drag physics, and an accessible shortest-path hint mechanism. |
| **Q3. Importance of Offline Play?** | **87.5%** (21/24) rated offline play as **"Very Important"** or **"Essential"**. | Local SQLite persistence for campaign levels and cached Daily Challenges is mandatory (Offline-First). |
| **Q4. Interest in Daily Challenges & Leaderboards?** | **70.8%** (17/24) stated a shared daily puzzle with a move-count leaderboard would motivate them to return daily. | Justifies building the Daily Challenge mode and Top-20 Leaderboard backed by Supabase. |
| **Q5. Audio preference during mobile play?** | **66.7%** (16/24) play muted in public/class but **75.0%** (18/24) enjoy subtle SFX + haptics at home. | Provide a persistent Mute/Unmute toggle accessible from both Home and Game screens, paired with `HapticFeedback`. |

### C. Qualitative Semi-Structured Interviews ($n = 5$ Participants)

Five participants from the survey pool completed 10-minute live think-aloud sessions while testing existing sliding-block puzzle apps:

| Participant | Profile | Observed Pain Point / Quote | Derived Requirement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **P1** | Daily bus commuter | *"When I'm dragging a car and hit another car, I want to feel a subtle bump instead of the car just freezing silently."* | Trigger `HapticFeedback.lightImpact()` + `horn_collide.mp3` when a vehicle reaches `minOffsetPx` or `maxOffsetPx`. |
| **P2** | Competitive puzzle fan | *"If I make one accidental swipe on a 20-move level, I don't want to restart the whole level—I just want to undo one step."* | Implement a full `Undo` stack (`BoardEngine.undo`) alongside `Reset`. |
| **P3** | Casual gamer | *"Sometimes I press Hint and the game freezes for a couple of seconds on hard levels, or gives me a weird detour."* | Run `BfsSolver` (guaranteed shortest path) inside `Isolate.run` with a loading indicator so the UI never freezes. |
| **P4** | Occasional player | *"If there's no internet on the subway and I open the Daily tab, the app shouldn't crash or show a blank white screen."* | Implement explicit **Loading (Shimmer), Content, Empty, and Error (with Retry)** states plus SQLite `daily_cache`. |
| **P5** | Color-vision-deficient user | *"In tight grids, I sometimes try to drag a vertical car sideways by mistake."* | Overlay subtle `↔` / `↕` directional indicators on every vehicle and enforce strict axis locking in `BoardEngine`. |
